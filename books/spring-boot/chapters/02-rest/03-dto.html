DTOs, mapping and Lombok

Never send entities over the wire. A DTO is the API's contract; the entity is the database's. Keep them separate and map between them.

In the franchise

The kitchen's stock register has supplier prices, margins and staff notes. The customer gets a printed menu with just names and prices. You'd never hand customers the stock register, and you can rearrange the kitchen without reprinting menus.

Security leaks

Serialising an entity exposes password hashes, internal flags and audit columns, and lets clients set fields they shouldn't (mass assignment).

Lazy-loading surprises

Jackson touches lazy collections: LazyInitializationException, N+1 queries, or infinite recursion between parent and child.

Coupled contract

Renaming a column changes your public API and breaks mobile apps.

Modern Java

Records already give you immutable DTOs with constructor, accessors, equals, hashCode and toString, so for DTOs you rarely need Lombok at all. Jackson 3 (in Boot 4) binds records out of the box.

Lock it in

Stock register stays in the kitchen; customers get the menu.

Request DTO in, response DTO out, entity in the middle. Records for DTOs, MapStruct for big mappings, no @Data on entities.

Interview questions

Why are records a good fit for DTOs?

They're immutable, concise, have value-based equals/hashCode/toString, work with Bean Validation annotations on components, and serialise/deserialise with Jackson without extra configuration.

How do you avoid infinite recursion when serialising bidirectional relationships?

Best: return DTOs that don't contain back-references. Workarounds on entities: @JsonManagedReference/@JsonBackReference or @JsonIgnore on one side.
