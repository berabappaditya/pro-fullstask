Microservices with Spring Boot and Spring Cloud

One service = one business capability = one database. A gateway in front, discovery (or Kubernetes DNS) between them, resilience on every call, events for decoupling, tracing across all.

In the franchise

Ramu's head office splits into separate counters with their own stock: Orders, Payments, Kitchen, Loyalty. A receptionist (API gateway) greets every visitor and sends them to the right counter. Counters talk by intercom for quick questions (REST) and by a notice board for announcements (Kafka). If Loyalty's line is busy, Orders doesn't wait forever (circuit breaker).

No shared database, so no cross-service @Transactional. Use the transactional outbox (write the event in the same DB transaction, relay it to Kafka) and sagas with compensating actions. Full explanation with diagrams: {{xref:system-design/ch-dtx}} and {{xref:system-design/ch-micro}}.

Interview questions

How does a circuit breaker work?

It wraps calls and counts failures over a sliding window. CLOSED: calls pass. When the failure rate crosses a threshold it goes OPEN: calls fail fast to the fallback without hitting the sick service. After a wait it goes HALF_OPEN and lets a few trial calls through; success closes it, failure re-opens. It stops cascading failures and thread-pool exhaustion.

When would you not use microservices?

Small teams, unclear domain boundaries, early-stage products, or when you can't invest in CI/CD, observability and on-call for many services. A well-modularised monolith is faster to build and can be split later along proven boundaries.
