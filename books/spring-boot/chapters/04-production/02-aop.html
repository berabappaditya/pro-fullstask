AOP and proxies: the hidden assistant behind the annotations

@Transactional, @Async, @Cacheable, @PreAuthorize and @Retryable all work the same way: Spring wraps your bean in a proxy that runs extra code around the method. Understand the proxy and you understand all their gotchas.

In the franchise

Busy chefs get a personal assistant who stands in front of them. Customers talk to the assistant, who logs the order, checks the card, starts the timer, passes the request to the chef, and stops the timer after. The chef only cooks. But when the chef talks to himself ("now I'll also make the sauce"), the assistant isn't involved: no log, no timer.

Annotation magic = proxy + advice. Proxies only see calls from outside, on public, non-final methods. Fix self-invocation by moving the method to another bean.

Interview questions

What is AOP and where does Spring use it?

Aspect-Oriented Programming separates cross-cutting concerns (transactions, security, caching, logging, metrics, retries) from business code. Spring AOP implements it with runtime proxies around beans; Spring's own @Transactional, @Async, @Cacheable and method security are built on it.

Spring AOP vs AspectJ?

Spring AOP is proxy-based, method-execution join points only, on Spring beans, woven at runtime: simple and enough for most needs. AspectJ weaves bytecode at compile or load time and supports fields, constructors, self-invocation and non-Spring objects, at the cost of build/agent setup.
