Cloud basics: service models, regions and shared responsibility

The cloud is renting computing on demand, paid by use. The more managed the service, the less you operate and the less you control.

In the food court

Ramu can rent an empty kitchen shell and bring his own stoves and staff (IaaS), rent a fully equipped kitchen where he just cooks (PaaS), or simply order from another restaurant and resell (SaaS). The food court has several buildings in each city (availability zones) and branches in many cities (regions), so a fire in one building doesn't close him down.

Regions, availability zones and edge locations

region
A geographic area

e.g. ap-south-1 (Mumbai), asia-south1 (GCP Mumbai). Choose by latency to users, data-residency law and service availability. Data doesn't leave a region unless you send it.

availability zone
Isolated data centres in a region

Separate power, cooling and network, a few km apart, connected by low-latency links. Spread across at least 2 AZs for high availability.

edge location
Points of presence near users

Hundreds of sites used by CDNs (CloudFront, Cloud CDN) and DNS to serve content close to users.

Interview questions

What is the shared responsibility model?

The provider is responsible for security of the cloud (data centres, hardware, hypervisor, managed-service internals). The customer is responsible for security in the cloud: IAM, network rules, OS patching on VMs, application code, data encryption choices and configuration. The line moves toward the provider as you use more managed services.

How do you choose a region?

Latency to most users, legal/data-residency requirements (e.g. Indian payment data rules), availability of the services you need, price differences between regions, and a second region for disaster recovery if required.
