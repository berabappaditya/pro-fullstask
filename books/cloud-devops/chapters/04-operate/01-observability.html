Observability, SLOs and alerting

Metrics tell you something is wrong, traces tell you where, logs tell you why. Alert on what users feel (SLOs), not on every CPU spike.

In the food court

Ramu doesn't stand at every counter. He watches a wall of gauges (metrics), follows a tracking number through every station when an order is late (traces), and reads the kitchen diary for that order (logs). His promise to customers: "99.5% of orders served within 5 minutes" (SLO). He only gets a phone call when that promise is at risk.

Good alerts

Page on symptoms: SLO burn rate (errors eating the budget fast), p99 latency above target, no successful orders in 5 minutes. Every alert has a runbook link.

Bad alerts

Page on causes that may not hurt users: one instance at 90% CPU, a single 500, disk at 70%. Those belong on dashboards or tickets.

Lock it in

Gauges, a tracking number, a diary, and one promise.

Metrics → traces → logs. RED for services, USE for resources. SLO 99.9% = 43 min/month budget. Alert on burn rate, with runbooks.

Interview questions

Your API's p99 latency doubled after a deploy. How do you investigate?

Confirm on dashboards (which endpoints, all instances or some, correlated with the deploy). Compare traces before/after to find the slow span (DB query, downstream call, GC). Check saturation: connection pool waits, CPU throttling, GC pauses. Check logs for errors/timeouts. If user impact is significant, roll back first, then investigate in staging with the same load.

What's an error budget used for?

It balances reliability and velocity: while budget remains, teams ship features and take risks; when it's exhausted, they freeze risky changes and invest in reliability. It turns "how reliable?" into an agreed number.
