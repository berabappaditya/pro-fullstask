Cloud security essentials

Most cloud breaches are misconfigurations: public buckets, leaked keys, over-broad permissions. Defend in layers: identity, network, data, workload, detection.

In the food court

Ramu's security isn't one big lock. Staff badges (identity), fences and back gates (network), a safe for cash with its own key held by head office (encryption with KMS), the recipe vault nobody copies into notebooks (secrets manager), CCTV that records who opened what (audit logs), and a guard who turns away troublemakers at the gate (WAF).

Interview questions

How do you store and rotate database credentials for a service?

Keep them in Secrets Manager / Secret Manager, encrypted with KMS; grant only the service's role read access to that one secret; inject at runtime (External Secrets, Spring Cloud AWS/GCP, CSI driver); enable automatic rotation and make the app re-read or use short-lived IAM database authentication where supported. Never bake into images or env files in Git.

What is envelope encryption?

Data is encrypted with a data key; the data key is encrypted with a master key in KMS that never leaves KMS. You store the encrypted data key next to the data. Decrypting needs a KMS call (permission-checked and audited) to unwrap the data key. It scales to large data and lets you rotate/revoke the master key centrally.
