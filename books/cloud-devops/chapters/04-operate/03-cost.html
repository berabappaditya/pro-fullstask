Cost optimisation: pay for what you actually use

Most cloud waste is idle or oversized resources, forgotten environments, and data transfer. Measure by team with tags, then right-size, commit, and automate.

In the food court

The first month's bill shocks Ramu: stoves left burning overnight in the test kitchen, a giant fridge holding one bottle of milk, and porters charging for every trip between buildings. He labels every appliance with the branch that uses it, switches off the test kitchen at night, buys smaller fridges, and signs a yearly contract for the stoves he always needs.

1 · see
Visibility

Tag everything (team, service, env); Cost Explorer / Billing reports; budgets and anomaly alerts.

2 · cut waste
Idle and orphaned

Unattached disks, old snapshots, idle load balancers, dev environments running at night (schedule them off), unused Elastic IPs.

3 · right-size
Fit the workload

Use utilisation data (Compute Optimizer / Recommender); Kubernetes requests that match reality; ARM (Graviton/Axion) instances.

4 · commit
Discounts

Savings Plans / committed-use discounts for the steady baseline; spot for interruptible work.

5 · architecture
Design for cost

Cache to cut DB size, S3 lifecycle tiers, VPC endpoints instead of NAT for S3 traffic, keep chatty services in one AZ/region, serverless for low traffic.

Hidden bills

NAT gateway data processing (pulling images and calling S3 through NAT), cross-AZ and internet egress, CloudWatch log ingestion from verbose DEBUG logs, high-cardinality custom metrics, provisioned IOPS you don't use, and forgotten snapshots.

Lock it in

Label, switch off, shrink, sign a contract for the basics.

Tag → find waste → right-size → commit (Savings Plans) → spot → design for cost. Watch data transfer and logs.

Interview questions

Your AWS bill jumped 40% this month. What do you do?

Break down by service, account and tag in Cost Explorer to find the driver; check anomalies (new resources, data transfer, NAT, logs); identify the owner via tags; fix the cause (scale down, lifecycle rules, endpoints, log levels). Then prevent: budgets with alerts, anomaly detection, tagging policies, and cost reviews in the team's routine.
