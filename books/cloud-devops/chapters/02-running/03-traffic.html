Getting traffic in: DNS, CDN and load balancers

DNS turns the name into an address, the CDN serves what it can from the edge, and the load balancer spreads the rest over healthy instances.

In the food court

A customer asks the information desk for "Ramu Chai" and is told "stall 42" (DNS). Bottled water and biscuits are sold at kiosks by every entrance so nobody walks all the way in (CDN). At stall 42, a host sends each customer to whichever counter is free and skips the one with a broken till (load balancer + health checks).

Interview questions

How do health checks and connection draining work during a deploy?

The LB probes each target's health endpoint; only healthy targets receive traffic. When an instance is being removed, it's deregistered: the LB stops sending new requests and waits (deregistration delay) for in-flight requests to finish while the app shuts down gracefully. Readiness probes play the same role in Kubernetes.

How do you invalidate CDN content after a deploy?

Prefer versioned filenames (app.3f9a1.js) with long max-age so new deploys reference new URLs and nothing needs purging; keep HTML short-lived. For emergencies use an invalidation (CloudFront invalidation / Cloud CDN cache invalidation), which can take minutes and may cost money.
