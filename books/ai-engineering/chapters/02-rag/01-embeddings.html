Embeddings: turning meaning into numbers

An embedding model turns a piece of text into a vector so that texts with similar meaning land close together. Search becomes "find the nearest vectors".

Ramu's assistant

Ramu's old filing cabinet sorted recipes alphabetically, so "Adrak chai" and "Ginger tea" were far apart even though they're the same drink. The assistant re-arranges them on a big map by taste: spicy teas in one corner, cold drinks in another, snacks elsewhere. Now "something warm with ginger" lands right next to both. That map is an embedding space.

Quality on your domain and languages (check MTEB-style benchmarks, then test on your data), dimensions (storage/speed), max input length, price, hosted vs local.

limits
What embeddings miss

Exact identifiers (order #A-7781, error codes), negation and numbers are weak spots. That's why hybrid search with keywords exists ({{n:ch-advanced-rag}}).

beyond text
Multimodal embeddings

Images and text in one space: "red sneakers" finds product photos.

Arrange recipes on a taste map, not alphabetically.

Text → vector; similar meaning = nearby vectors; compare with cosine. Same model for queries and documents. Weak on exact IDs and numbers.

Interview questions

What is an embedding and why is it useful?

A dense vector representation produced by a model so that semantic similarity becomes geometric closeness. It enables semantic search, recommendations, clustering, deduplication and classification, and is the retrieval backbone of RAG.
