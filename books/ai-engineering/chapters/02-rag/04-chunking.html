Chunking and ingestion: garbage in, garbage retrieved

Split documents into pieces small enough to be specific and large enough to make sense on their own, keep useful metadata, and parse messy formats carefully.

Ramu's assistant

If the handbook is one giant page, every lookup returns the whole book (too vague). If it's cut into single sentences, "they" and "this rule" lose their meaning (too fragmented). Ramu cuts it by section, keeps the section title on each piece, and lets pieces overlap a little so no rule is cut in half.

sizes
Starting point

200–800 tokens with 10–20% overlap, then tune with your evaluation set. Smaller for precise FAQs, larger for narrative docs.

metadata
Keep it

Source, title, section path, page, date, tenant/ACL, language, version. Enables filtering, citations and freshness.

pitfalls
Ingestion failures

Scanned PDFs without OCR, tables flattened into nonsense, headers/footers repeated in every chunk, duplicate documents, stale versions never removed.

Lock it in

Cut by section, keep the title, overlap a little.

Structural/recursive splitting as default, rich metadata, parent–child for precision + context, re-ingest idempotently, and evaluate chunk settings.

Interview questions

How do you choose chunk size?

Balance specificity (small chunks match precise questions) against self-containedness (large chunks keep context). Start from the document structure and ~500 tokens with overlap, then run the evaluation set comparing retrieval recall/precision and answer quality across sizes. Consider parent–child retrieval to get both.
