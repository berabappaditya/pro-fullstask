Advanced RAG: hybrid search, reranking, query transformation, GraphRAG and agentic RAG

Naive RAG fails on exact terms, vague questions and multi-hop questions. Each advanced technique fixes one specific failure.

Ramu's assistant

The assistant now checks both the index at the back of the book (keywords) and the taste map (vectors), then re-reads the top 20 pages carefully to pick the best 5 (reranking). When a customer asks something vague, he first rephrases it into a clear question (query rewriting). For "which supplier delivers to the branch that had the most complaints?", he looks things up in several hops (agentic / graph RAG).

Long context vs RAG

With million-token context windows, "just put everything in the prompt" works for small, static corpora and one-off analysis. RAG still wins for large or changing knowledge, per-user access control, cost and latency per question, and citations. Many systems combine them: retrieve generously, then let a long-context model read more.

Interview questions

What is reranking and why does it help?

Vector search (bi-encoder) embeds query and documents separately: fast but coarse. A reranker (cross-encoder) reads the query and each candidate together and scores relevance much more accurately, but is too slow for millions of docs. So retrieve 20–50 cheaply, rerank, keep the top 3–5: better precision and shorter prompts.

When would you use GraphRAG?

When questions depend on relationships and aggregation across many documents ("which suppliers are linked to complaints in branches managed by X?", "main themes across all reports"). It builds an entity–relationship graph during ingestion (costly) and retrieves by traversal or community summaries. For simple fact lookup, standard hybrid RAG is cheaper and enough.
