Vector databases and approximate nearest-neighbour search

Comparing a query with millions of vectors one by one is too slow; ANN indexes like HNSW find the nearest ones in milliseconds by trading a little accuracy.

Ramu's assistant

To find the dish nearest to "spicy and warm" on a map with a million dishes, the assistant doesn't measure every dish. He uses highways between regions, then local roads, then walks the last street (HNSW's layers). He might occasionally miss the very closest one, but he's a thousand times faster.

Interview questions

Do you need a dedicated vector database?

Often not. pgvector in an existing Postgres handles millions of vectors with HNSW, keeps data and permissions transactional with your business tables, and avoids a new system. Dedicated vector DBs help at very large scale (hundreds of millions+), with heavy filtering at high QPS, or when you want managed sharding and hybrid features out of the box.

What is recall in ANN search and how do you tune it?

The fraction of the true nearest neighbours the index returns. HNSW: higher M (links per node) and ef_construction improve index quality; higher ef_search improves recall per query at the cost of latency. Measure against exact search on a sample.
