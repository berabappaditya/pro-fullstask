How LLMs work: tokens, embeddings, attention and training

An LLM predicts the next token given all the tokens so far, then repeats. Everything it "knows" comes from patterns learned in training; everything it "sees" now is in its context window.

Ramu's assistant

Ask the assistant "Masala chai needs tea, milk, sugar and…" and it says "ginger" because it has read thousands of recipes, not because it looked anything up. It writes one word, re-reads everything including that word, writes the next, and so on. It only remembers what's on the desk in front of it (the context window); yesterday's conversation is gone unless you put it back on the desk.

How a model gets trained

  1. Pre-trainingPredict the next token on trillions of tokens of web text, books and code. The model learns language, facts and reasoning patterns. Months on thousands of GPUs; knowledge freezes at a cutoff date.
  2. Instruction tuning (SFT)Train on example conversations so it follows instructions and answers helpfully instead of just continuing text.
  3. Preference training (RLHF / RLAIF / DPO)Humans or AI feedback rank answers; the model is tuned toward preferred, safer, more honest responses. Reasoning models add reinforcement learning on verifiable tasks (maths, code).

Interview questions

Why do LLMs hallucinate?

They're trained to produce likely continuations, not verified facts. When the needed fact is missing, rare in training data, after the cutoff or ambiguous, the most plausible-sounding tokens may be wrong, and the model has no built-in signal of its own uncertainty. Grounding with retrieved sources, asking for citations, allowing "I don't know", tool use for live data, and evaluation reduce it.

What is attention, in one minute?

For each token, the model computes how relevant every other (earlier) token is (query–key similarity) and builds a weighted mix of their information (values). Many attention heads in many layers let it track grammar, references ("it" → "the order") and long-range meaning. Cost grows with the square of sequence length, which is why long contexts are expensive.
