Reliability and fault tolerance

Everything fails eventually. Reliable systems expect failure and are designed so one broken part doesn't take down the rest.

At Ramu's chai stall

Ramu keeps a spare gas cylinder (redundancy). When the milk supplier is late, he doesn't make customers wait forever; after 5 minutes he serves black tea instead (timeout and fallback). When the supplier has been late three days in a row, Ramu stops calling him altogether for a week and buys from the shop next door, checking back occasionally (circuit breaker). And he keeps the samosa fryer on a separate gas line, so a fryer problem can't cut the gas to the tea stove (bulkhead).

The resilience toolkit

Redundancy

No single point of failure: at least two of everything, across availability zones. Active–active (both serve) or active–passive (standby takes over).

Timeouts

Never wait forever. Every network call gets a timeout shorter than the caller's own deadline.

Retries with backoff + jitter

Retry transient errors, waiting 100 ms, 200 ms, 400 ms… plus randomness, so clients don't retry in sync.

Circuit breaker

After repeated failures, stop calling a sick service for a while and fail fast.

Bulkhead

Separate thread pools/connection pools per dependency so one slow dependency can't exhaust everything.

Fallback & graceful degradation

Show cached recommendations, hide the reviews widget, but keep checkout working.

Load shedding

When overloaded, reject low-priority work early (503) to keep high-priority work alive.

Idempotency

Makes retries safe. Without it, retries cause duplicates.

Disaster recovery: RPO and RTO

RPO
Recovery Point Objective

How much data can you afford to lose? "At most 5 minutes of orders." Drives backup/replication frequency.

RTO
Recovery Time Objective

How long can you be down? "Back within 1 hour." Drives standby strategy: backup-restore → pilot light → warm standby → multi-region active-active.

Lock it in

"Two of everything, Time out, Back off, Break the circuit, Build bulkheads."

Retries without timeouts, backoff + jitter and idempotency make outages worse. The circuit breaker is a fuse: Closed → Open → Half-open. Always have a degraded mode.

Interview questions

What is a cascading failure and how do you prevent it?

One slow service causes its callers' threads to pile up waiting, so they slow down, and their callers too, until everything is down. Prevent with timeouts on every call, circuit breakers, bulkheads (separate pools), load shedding and async communication where possible.

Why add jitter to exponential backoff?

Without jitter, all clients that failed at the same moment retry at the same moments (100 ms, 200 ms, 400 ms…), creating synchronized waves that hit the recovering service. Randomness spreads retries out.
