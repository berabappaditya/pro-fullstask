Back-of-the-envelope estimation

Rough maths that tells you whether you need 1 server or 1,000, one database or a sharded cluster, before you draw a single box.

At Ramu's chai stall

Before opening at the new airport, Ramu estimates: 30,000 passengers a day, maybe 1 in 10 buys chai, so 3,000 cups. Most come in the 6–9 a.m. rush, so roughly 1,000 cups an hour at peak, about 17 a minute. One person can serve 4 a minute, so he needs 5 counter staff in the morning. He didn't need exact numbers, just the right order of magnitude.

Numbers to carry in your head

Time
  • 1 day = 86,400 s ≈ 105 s
  • 1 month ≈ 2.5 million s
  • 1 year ≈ 3 × 107 s
Data sizes
  • KB = 103, MB = 106, GB = 109, TB = 1012, PB = 1015 bytes
  • 210 ≈ 1 thousand, 220 ≈ 1 million, 230 ≈ 1 billion
Typical sizes
  • A tweet-like text row: ~300 B – 1 KB
  • Compressed photo: ~200 KB – 2 MB
  • 1 minute of HD video: ~50–100 MB
Rough capacity
  • One app server: ~1,000–5,000 simple req/s
  • One MySQL: a few thousand writes/s, ~10K+ simple reads/s
  • One Redis node: ~100K ops/s

Worked example: "ChaiTalk", a short-post social app

Assumptions (always state them aloud): 50 million daily active users; each posts 2 times and reads 100 posts a day; a post is ~500 bytes; 10% of posts carry a 200 KB photo; read:write ratio is high; peak traffic is about 3× average.

QuantityCalculationResult
Write QPS (avg)50M × 2 = 100M posts/day ÷ 105 s~1,000 /s (peak ~3,000)
Read QPS (avg)50M × 100 = 5B reads/day ÷ 105 s~50,000 /s (peak ~150,000)
Text storage per day100M × 500 B50 GB/day → ~18 TB/year
Photo storage per day10M × 200 KB2 TB/day → ~730 TB/year
App servers at peak150,000 ÷ ~1,500 per server~100 servers
Cache for hot posts20% of a day's posts: 0.2 × 50 GB~10 GB → a small Redis cluster
Photo egress50K reads/s × 20% with photo × 200 KB~2 GB/s → must use a CDN

What the numbers told us: reads dominate 50:1, so design for reads (caching, replicas, precomputed feeds). Text fits a sharded database; photos go to object storage with a CDN. 100 servers means a load balancer and stateless services. That's the whole architecture's skeleton, from five minutes of arithmetic.

Interview tip

Round aggressively (86,400 → 100,000), write the steps on the board, and say what each number implies for the design. Interviewers grade reasoning, not precision. Don't spend more than about 5 minutes here.

Lock it in

A day is 105 seconds. Users × actions ÷ 105 = QPS. Peak ≈ 2–3×.

Then storage = items/day × size × retention, and bandwidth = QPS × size. Every number should end with "…so we need X".
