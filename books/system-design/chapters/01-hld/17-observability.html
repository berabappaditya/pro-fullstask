Observability: logs, metrics, traces and SLOs

Observability means you can tell what your system is doing, and why, from the outside, without adding new code every time something breaks.

At Ramu's chai stall

With 40 branches, Ramu can't watch every stove. So he installs a dashboard showing cups sold per minute and average wait time (metrics), keeps a complaints diary at every branch (logs), and gives each big catering order a token number that every counter writes down, so he can see exactly where order #7781 got stuck (tracing). He also promises customers "95% of chai served within 3 minutes" (SLO).

pillar 1
Logs

Timestamped records of events: "payment failed for order 7781: card declined". Structured JSON, centralised (ELK, Loki, CloudWatch). Answer "what exactly happened?"

pillar 2
Metrics

Numbers over time: requests/s, error %, p99 latency, CPU. Cheap to store, great for dashboards and alerts (Prometheus + Grafana). Answer "is something wrong?"

pillar 3
Traces

One request's journey across services, as a tree of timed spans sharing a trace ID (OpenTelemetry, Jaeger, Zipkin). Answer "where is it slow?"

SLI, SLO, SLA and the error budget

SLI · indicator
What you measure

"Percentage of requests faster than 300 ms", "percentage of successful requests".

SLO · objective
Your internal target

"99.9% of requests succeed over 30 days."

SLA · agreement
The contract with customers

"99.5% uptime or you get a 10% refund." Always looser than the SLO.

error budget
Allowed failure

99.9% SLO = 0.1% budget ≈ 43 minutes/month. Budget left? Ship features. Budget spent? Focus on reliability.

The four golden signals (Google SRE): Latency, Traffic, Errors, Saturation. Alert on symptoms users feel (error rate, latency SLO burn), not on every CPU spike.

Lock it in

Metrics say "something's wrong", traces say "where", logs say "why".

Golden signals: Latency, Traffic, Errors, Saturation. SLI is the measurement, SLO the target, SLA the promise with penalties; the gap is your error budget.

Interview questions

What would you put on the dashboard for a new service?

Request rate, error rate (by status code), latency p50/p95/p99 per endpoint, saturation (CPU, memory, thread pool and DB connection pool usage, queue lag), dependency health (calls, errors, latency to DB/cache/other services), and business metrics (orders per minute). Alerts on SLO burn rate rather than raw thresholds.
