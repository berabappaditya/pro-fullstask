Replication: copies of the same data

Replication keeps the same data on several machines so that reads scale, and a dead machine doesn't lose data or stop the service.

At Ramu's chai stall

Ramu now has branches in Kharagpur and Kolkata. Only the head office ledger can be written to (new orders, price changes). Every evening a clerk photocopies the changes and sends them to each branch, so branches can look up prices and loyalty points locally without calling head office.

One day a customer pays at head office and immediately walks into the Kolkata branch asking for his points. The photocopy hasn't arrived yet, so the branch shows his old balance. That's replication lag.

Synchronous vs asynchronous

Synchronous

The leader waits until a follower confirms before saying "committed". No data loss on failover, but every write is as slow as the slowest replica, and a dead replica blocks writes.

Asynchronous

The leader commits immediately and ships changes later. Fast writes, but followers lag, and if the leader dies, the last few writes may be lost.

Semi-synchronous

Wait for at least one follower, the rest async. The common middle ground (MySQL semi-sync, Postgres synchronous_standby_names).

Three replication topologies

Quorum formula

With N replicas, a write waits for W acks and a read asks R replicas. If R + W > N, at least one replica in every read has the latest write. Typical: N=3, W=2, R=2. Tune for speed: W=1 (fast writes, risky) or R=1 (fast reads).

Failover: when the leader dies

  1. DetectFollowers or a monitor (Orchestrator, Patroni, cloud RDS) notice missed heartbeats, typically after 10–30 seconds.
  2. ElectPick the most up-to-date follower as the new leader (consensus such as Raft, or a coordinator like ZooKeeper/etcd).
  3. ReconfigurePoint writes at the new leader (DNS/virtual IP/proxy update); other followers now follow it.
  4. Fence the old leaderIf the old leader comes back thinking it's still in charge, you get split brain: two leaders accepting writes. Fencing tokens or STONITH ("shoot the other node in the head") prevent it.

One pen, many photocopies.

Replication = same data, many machines (for availability and read scale). Sharding = different data, many machines (for write and storage scale). Async replication is fast but laggy; quorum rule R + W > N; beware split brain on failover.

Interview questions

Replication vs backup: why do you need both?

Replication copies every change immediately, including mistakes: a DELETE without a WHERE is replicated to every follower in milliseconds. Backups (and point-in-time recovery from logs) let you go back in time. Replication protects against machine failure; backups protect against human and software error.

A user updates their name and immediately sees the old one. Why, and how do you fix it?

The write went to the leader but the read was served by a lagging replica. Fixes: read the user's own profile from the leader for a few seconds after an edit; or record the leader's log position after the write and only read from replicas that have reached it; or update the UI optimistically on the client.
