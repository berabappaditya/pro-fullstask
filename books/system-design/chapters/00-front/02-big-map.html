The big map: what is system design?

System design is deciding the parts of a software system and how they talk to each other so that it stays fast, correct and cheap as it grows.

At Ramu's chai stall

Ramu sells tea from a single cart near Medinipur station. He boils the water, takes the money and remembers who wants less sugar. For 50 customers a day, that's perfect.

Then a food blogger posts about his elaichi chai. Next morning, 2,000 people turn up. The kettle is too small, the queue blocks the road, he forgets orders, and the cash box overflows. Nothing about his tea changed. His system broke because the load changed. That is exactly the problem system design solves.

System design questions come at two zoom levels. Think of building a city:

The four questions behind every design

Scalability

Can it handle 10× or 100× more users by adding machines?

Availability

Does it keep working when a machine, a disk or a whole data centre dies?

Performance

How fast is one request (latency), and how many per second can it serve (throughput)?

Maintainability

Can new developers understand and change it without breaking things? This is where LLD and SOLID live.

Lock it in

HLD = the city. LLD = the rooms. SOLID = the building code.

Every design is a trade-off. There is no "best" architecture, only the best one for these requirements. Saying "it depends, and here is what it depends on" is the most senior sentence in system design.

Quick check

What is the difference between HLD and LLD in one sentence each?

HLD describes the major components of a system (services, databases, caches, queues) and how data flows between them. LLD describes the internals of one component: its classes, interfaces, relationships and methods.

Why do systems that work fine on day one break later?

Because load, data size and team size grow. A design that ignores scale, failure and change works only until one of those grows past what a single machine or a single tangled codebase can handle.
