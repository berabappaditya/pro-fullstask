LLD project: a banking system

Accounts, deposits, withdrawals and transfers that are correct under concurrency, auditable to the paisa, and safe to retry. The favourite LLD question for fintech and payments interviews.

At Ramu's chai stall

Ramu's chain now runs a small staff savings scheme. Two cashiers, at the same second, process "Bablu sends ₹500 to Gopal" and "Gopal sends ₹200 to Bablu". Each cashier grabs one passbook and waits for the other's. Neither lets go. The queue stops. That's a deadlock. Ramu's rule from then on: always pick up the passbook with the smaller account number first. Nobody can ever end up holding one while waiting for the other in a circle.

Step 1 · Requirements

Functional
  • Customers open savings, current and fixed-deposit accounts
  • Deposit, withdraw, transfer between accounts
  • Statement: full history of every movement
  • Account states: active, frozen, closed
  • Monthly interest for savings accounts
  • SMS/email alert on every debit
Rules and guarantees
  • Balance never goes below zero (or below the overdraft limit for current accounts)
  • Daily withdrawal/transfer limit per account
  • A retried transfer never moves money twice
  • Concurrent transfers never deadlock or lose updates
  • Money is never created or destroyed: every debit has a matching credit

Step 2 · Key design decisions

value object
Money, not double

BigDecimal with currency, immutable. 0.1 + 0.2 != 0.3 in floating point; banks can't round randomly.

audit
Double-entry ledger

Every transfer writes two ledger entries (debit A, credit B) that sum to zero. The balance is a cached total of the ledger; the ledger is the truth.

LSP
Not every account can be debited

Fixed deposits can't be withdrawn from, so only TransactionalAccount has debit() ({{ref:ch-lsp}}).

concurrency
Lock in a global order

Always lock the lower account ID first, with a timeout. No circular wait, no deadlock.

idempotency
Every transfer has a key

Same key → same result; the transfer runs at most once ({{ref:ch-stripe}}).

OCP
Rules and interest are pluggable

Transfer rules form a chain; interest calculation is a strategy; alerts are observers.

Interview questions

What are the four conditions for deadlock, and which one does lock ordering break?

Mutual exclusion, hold and wait, no preemption, and circular wait (Coffman conditions). A global lock order breaks circular wait: if everyone acquires locks in the same order, a cycle can't form. tryLock with a timeout additionally breaks "wait forever".

Why store a ledger instead of just updating a balance column?

A balance alone can't explain itself. The ledger is an append-only history: it gives statements, audits, dispute resolution, and a way to rebuild or verify the balance. The balance is a cached projection of the ledger, kept in the same transaction.
