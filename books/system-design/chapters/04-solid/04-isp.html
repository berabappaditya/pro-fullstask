Interface Segregation Principle

No client should be forced to depend on methods it doesn't use. Many small, focused interfaces beat one fat one.

At Ramu's chai stall

Ramu's hiring form had one job description for everyone: "Must brew tea, drive the delivery van, and maintain the accounts ledger." The cook can't drive, the driver can't do accounts, and the accountant can't make tea, so every new hire had to fake two-thirds of the job. Ramu replaced it with three short role cards: Brewer, Driver, Accountant. His nephew, who can brew and drive, simply holds two cards.

Definition

Split large interfaces into smaller, role-specific ones so that implementers only implement what they can genuinely do, and callers only depend on what they genuinely use. SRP applied to interfaces.

Interview tip

Spring Data shows ISP in layers: Repository (marker) → CrudRepository → PagingAndSortingRepository → JpaRepository. You can also declare a tiny custom interface extending Repository<Order, Long> with only findById and save, so the service can't call deleteAll() by mistake.

Lock it in

Role cards, not one giant job description.

If an implementer throws UnsupportedOperationException or leaves methods empty, the interface is too fat. Split by who uses it.

Interview questions

Doesn't ISP lead to too many interfaces?

It can if applied blindly. Split along real client needs: if every client uses all methods together, one interface is right. Split when distinct groups of clients use distinct subsets, or when some implementers can't support some methods.
