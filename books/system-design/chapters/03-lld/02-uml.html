UML class diagrams, relationships, cohesion and coupling

A class diagram is the floor plan of your code. Six kinds of lines describe how classes relate, and two words, cohesion and coupling, describe whether the plan is any good.

At Ramu's chai stall

An order is made of order lines ("2 masala chai"). Tear up the order slip and its lines are gone too: that's composition. A stall has employees, but if the stall closes, Bablu still exists and joins another stall: that's aggregation. A customer places orders; both exist independently: association. The billing clerk borrows a calculator for a moment and gives it back: dependency.

Cohesion and coupling: the two quality dials

Cohesion: how focused is one module?

High cohesion = everything inside a class is about one thing. A TaxCalculator that only calculates tax. Want it high.

Coupling: how tangled are modules?

Low coupling = classes know little about each other and talk through small interfaces. Change one, the others don't care. Want it low.

Every SOLID principle in the next part is, at heart, a technique for raising cohesion and lowering coupling.

Interview questions

Composition vs aggregation: give a real example of each.

Composition: House and Room; destroy the house and its rooms cease to exist; rooms aren't shared. In code, the whole creates and exclusively owns the parts. Aggregation: Team and Player; the team disbands but players continue and may belong to other teams. In code, parts are created elsewhere and passed in.

How do you reduce coupling between two classes?

Depend on an interface instead of a concrete class; inject dependencies rather than creating them with new; expose fewer public methods; communicate via events where a direct call isn't needed; follow the Law of Demeter (don't reach through objects: a.getB().getC().doX()).
